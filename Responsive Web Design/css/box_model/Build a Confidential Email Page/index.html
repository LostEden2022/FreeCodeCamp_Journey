The U.S. Army Special Forces, commonly known as the Green Berets, are an elite branch of the military specializing in unconventional warfare. Established in the early days of the Cold War, they are trained to operate independently behind enemy lines, wearing their iconic headwear as a symbol of distinction, resilience, and operational excellence.

Unlike standard infantry units, Green Berets are organized into small, highly autonomous Operational Detachments-Alpha (ODAs). Their core missions include foreign internal defense, special reconnaissance, counter-terrorism, and direct action, often embedding with and training local allied forces in complex, high-risk environments worldwide.

Becoming a Green Beret requires enduring one of the most grueling training pipelines in the military, known as the "Q-Course". Candidates must master advanced tactical skills, survival techniques, and foreign languages, proving they possess the exceptional mental fortitude and cultural adaptability required to lead under extreme pressure.
